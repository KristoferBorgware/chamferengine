One pixel, many cells

The planet is a honeycomb of cells on a ball. To look at the whole of it at once you have to draw it flat, and a flat drawing has a fixed number of pixels while the ball has a great many more cells. Something has to give. Which cells a pixel shows is the whole question.

Subdivision

Keep one cell

Each pixel ends up holding whichever cell landed on it last. The others are thrown away.

Average the cells

Each pixel is the mean of every cell that landed on it, so no cell is thrown away.

Both pictures are the same field on the same cells, projected the same way. Only what a pixel does with the cells that reach it differs. Walk the subdivision up and watch them separate: at level 5 there are fewer cells than pixels and the two agree, with gaps where nothing reached. By level 8 there are ten cells to a pixel, and the picture that keeps one of them is speckle while the picture that averages them is a planet. More cells is more information, and one of these two throws it away.
