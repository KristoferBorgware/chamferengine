D = round(log2(1.20459 × radius ÷ block))  = 13

Neither number is chosen directly, and neither can change afterwards. A cell's address spells out the route down D splits, so moving D would rename every cell in the world. D stops at 17, where the address word runs out of bits — at the settings above that is a block.

The rule is one line: a triangle is drawn when you are at least two of its own widths away from it, and split into its four children when you are closer. A chunk one step coarser is twice as wide, so it has to be twice as far away to be drawn: every step doubles the cell and doubles the distance. The ring distances here are the ones selectChunks produces — at 32 cells and 1 m blocks it puts the first step at 154 m, and this page says 154 m.
Chunk decides where the steps are, not how detailed the world is. Halving the cells in a chunk halves the chunk's width, which pulls every step ring in by half and puts coarse ground much closer to you. The block size is untouched: the world still has 1 m cells, you just have to walk to them.
