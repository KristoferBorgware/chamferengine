Walk, and the sky moves

On a planet 1,700 m in radius, up is a different direction at every step. Walk far enough and the whole celestial sphere goes past overhead — in 2.12 hours, without waiting for anything. Drag the walker and watch the right-hand view.

The left panel is a slice through the planet: the blue arrow is the walker's own up, which is just normalize(position). The right panel is what they see — the whole visible sky flattened into a dome, so the zenith is the top and both horizons are the ends. The moon is drawn to scale — which is why 0.52° is a speck, and why its size has to be an art decision. It is at a finite distance, so it slides against the stars as you walk; that shift is parallax, and a skybox painted at infinity does not have it.
