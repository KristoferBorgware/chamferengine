# v0.1.1

Patch: fixes to what 0.1.0 shipped. No item adds a capability.

Worked through in the three steps
[`HOW-TO-WRITE-PLANS.md`](../HOW-TO-WRITE-PLANS.md) sets out — discuss, try,
implement. Steps 1 and 2 run per item; step 3 runs once, for the whole release,
and starts when every item in the scope has cleared step 2.

**The release is about scale.** Everything in it came from standing on the
ground and finding that nothing reads at the size it should: the sky is used up
within two metres of altitude, the clouds are a dome you can touch, and the
ground has hills where it should have landforms.

**Old share codes stop working.** I-3 changes the subdivision depth, and a share
code carries the address at a depth. Nothing is saved yet, so nothing is lost,
but every link made from 0.1.0 names a different place after this release.

## Items

### I-1 — The atmosphere is used up by two metres of altitude

**From:** visual inspection, 2026-08-18 — "the atmosphere is very close and
extremely thin, so thin that you can only see the sky scattering at a certain
precise altitude"
**Findings pulled in:** F-011
**Step:** 1

#### The problem

`SKY_SHADER.ts` multiplies the camera's altitude by `6371000 / 1700 = 3747.6`
before placing it in the atmosphere it draws.

| Height here | Lands at on Earth | |
|---|---|---|
| 0 m | 0 km | |
| 1.6 m, eye height | 6.0 km | already a mountain-top sky |
| 16 m | 60 km | above the atmosphere |
| 150 m, the tallest ground | 562 km | far above it |

**Half the air is gone by 1.48 m of altitude**, and the whole 60 km atmosphere
is used up by 16 m. The ground reaches 150 m, so terrain stands above the sky
that lights it. The window where the sky reads correctly is roughly 0 to 2 m.

[Doc 32](../docs/32-sky-clouds-and-moon.md) decided to run the scattering on a
fictional Earth-sized atmosphere and take **only the sun direction** from the
world. It never asked for the camera's height to be scaled. That multiply
arrived with Project 12 and contradicts the document.

#### What both candidates have to carry

**The atmosphere is a property of the planet, not a constant in a shader.** It
is currently written down twice and parameterised neither time:

- `packages/engine/src/sky/ATMOSPHERE.ts` holds Earth's numbers in TypeScript
  and is read by nothing but its own test.
- `SKY_SHADER.ts` holds `PLANET`, `TOP`, `H_RAY`, `H_MIE`, `B_RAY`, `B_MIE` and
  `G_MIE` as WGSL `const` declarations, which is what actually draws and which
  cannot be changed while the program runs.

The next world may be Mars-like. Whatever is chosen below, the numbers reach the
shader through the uniform it already has — `settings : vec4f` uses two of its
four slots today.

#### Candidates

**B1 — Earth's atmosphere, with a per-planet mapping.** The world declares a sky
scale; the shader lifts the camera's real altitude by it into the modelled air.
A Mars-like world gets its own coefficients, its own scale height and its own
scale factor.

**B2 — The planet declares its own atmosphere, in world metres.** An atmosphere
top, a scale height, and scattering coefficients raised so the zenith optical
depth lands where it is wanted. No mapping and no lift: the camera's real height
goes straight in, and the air ends at the altitude the world says it ends at.

#### What separates them

The **horizon-to-zenith path ratio**, `sqrt(2*pi*R/H)`, which is what reddens a
sunset and keeps the zenith darker than the horizon.

| Atmosphere | Scale height | Ratio |
|---|---|---|
| Earth | 8 km | 70.7 |
| Scaled to this planet | 2.1 m | 70.8 |
| 200 m tall | 26.7 m | 20.0 |
| 400 m tall | 53.3 m | 14.2 |
| 800 m tall | 106.7 m | 10.0 |

The first two rows are the finding. **An atmosphere scaled in proportion with
the planet reproduces Earth's sky geometry exactly**, because the ratio depends
on `R/H` and `H` scales with `R`. Doc 32 is right that this needs air 3,748
times denser, and the density is invented in either candidate; what the document
does not say is that the scaled version is geometrically identical. Proportional
scaling does not fail on the physics. It fails because a **16 m** atmosphere is
one a player walks out of.

So B2's trade is stated plainly: **choosing how tall the air is chooses how
strong sunsets are.** B1 keeps Earth's sunsets on every world forever, which for
a thin-aired planet is wrong in the other direction.

#### Trial

Not started. A demo with the sun near the horizon and a slider on atmosphere
height settles it, because both candidates are correct arithmetic and the
question is which sky is worth having.

**F-011 applies:** WebGPU cannot present in the development container, so this
trial is a demo run on real hardware, not a screenshot.

### I-2 — The clouds are a dome, and refilling them stalls the frame

**From:** visual inspection, 2026-08-18 — "the clouds are just too close, they
should be further up, and they feel like textures on a dome"
**Findings pulled in:** F-014, F-011
**Step:** 1

#### The problem

`buildCloudMesh` emits **one flat hexagon per point at one radius**. No
thickness, no sides, no bottom. It is a texture on a dome, and it is drawn as
one.

The deck sits at 220 m, which is 1.47 times the tallest ground, and three
seconds of flying away. The furthest cloud visible from eye height is 896 m;
the same measurement on Earth is 143 km.

**And the field already stalls the frame.** Refilling it costs **12.7 ms on the
thread that draws, every 0.7 seconds** — three quarters of a frame's budget,
twice a second. That is F-014, and it exists today with flat clouds.

| Level | Puff | Points on the sky | Carrying cloud | Refill, one layer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5, in use | 64 m | 10,242 | 2,813 (27%) | 12.7 ms |
| 6 | 32 m | 40,962 | 11,263 (27%) | 52.7 ms |
| 7 | 16 m | 163,842 | 45,051 (27%) | 192.6 ms |

#### What both candidates have to carry

- **Two decks**, raised, with smaller puffs.
- **The field and its mesh are built on a worker.** Everything asked for is
  unaffordable on the main thread — level 6 over four layers is 211 ms — and
  the field is already a pure function of the wind angle and the seed, so it
  moves the way chunks moved. This closes F-014.

Raising the deck buys more sky than distance:

| Deck height | Seen to | Share of sky in reach | Puffs in view, level 6 | level 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 220 m, today | 896 m | 5.7% | 2,347 | 9,387 |
| 600 m | 1,330 m | 13.0% | 5,343 | 21,371 |
| 900 m | 1,533 m | 17.3% | 7,090 | 28,357 |
| 1,400 m | 1,757 m | 22.6% | 9,249 | 36,997 |

Going from 220 m to 900 m triples the sky within reach and lifts the viewing
distance only 1.7 times. That is the right gain here: the complaint is about
seeing the dome's edge, not about seeing far.

#### Candidates

**A — Hex prisms.** One point per column, with a base radius and a top radius
from the noise, so a puff has height and a silhouette. **6.4 times today's
triangles**, and one noise evaluation per point as now. Flat-topped within a
puff, so a plateau rather than a billow.

**B — Cloud cells over shells.** Sample the noise in three dimensions over four
to eight shells and emit the exposed faces, culling the interior ones the way
the terrain mesher culls them. **12.7 times the triangles over four shells, 22.2
over eight.** A cloud then has a shape from below, from the side, and from
inside. Four to eight times the build cost of a prism.

Triangles decide nothing: 570,000 for the whole sky at level 6 over four shells
is one terrain view, and frustum culling already cuts it. **The build cost is
the whole question**, which is why the worker move comes first.

#### What B needs said out loud

Doc 32 holds that a cloud has **no address** — no cell ID, no chunk, no layer —
and that withholding it is what keeps clouds cosmetic by construction. B builds
clouds out of things shaped like cells at shells.

It is compatible, and the reason is exact: what makes a thing storable is the
**cell ID**, because the delta store, the side table, interest routing and edit
messages are all keyed by it. A mesher taking a face, an offset and a shell, and
returning triangles, never mints one. If B wins, doc 32 gains a sentence saying
so, or its next reader will believe the rule was broken.

#### Trial

Not started. **F-011 applies** — this is a demo on real hardware.

### I-3 — The ground has no landforms at the size a person reads

**From:** visual inspection, 2026-08-18 — "the landscape has no variation, no
mountains, no valleys"
**Findings pulled in:** F-010
**Step:** 1

#### The problem

The terrain is **not proportionally flat**. 131 m of relief on a 10.7 km
circumference is 1 : 82, against Earth's 1 : 2004 — so this planet is already 25
times more rugged than Earth for its size. Adding amplitude fights that reading
rather than fixing it.

What is wrong is the size of a landform against the size of a person, and how
far a person can see:

| Tier | Longest feature | Shortest | Height it carries |
|---|---|---|---|
| Continent | 2,125 m | 266 m | 29.3 m sd |
| Relief | 283 m | 18 m | 10.2 m sd |
| Detail | 28 m | 4 m | 1.2 m sd |

The tallest ground measured is **54 m — thirty people**. An Earth mountain is
five hundred to sixteen hundred. And the horizon at eye height is **74 m**, so
a landform wider than about 150 m can never be seen whole from the ground: a
200 m peak at 74 m is a wall, not a view.

#### The decision taken

**The planet grows to a 6,800 m radius at subdivision depth 13**, which holds
the block at 1.000 m and doubles the horizon to 147 m. The alternative — keeping
the planet and spending the amplitude — is recorded as the candidate not taken.

| Radius | Horizon | Walk round | Depth for 1 m blocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.7 km | 74 m | 2.1 h | 11 |
| 6.8 km | 147 m | 8.5 h | 13 |
| 25 km | 283 m | 31 h | 15 |
| 111 km | 596 m | 138 h | 18, over the 17-level ceiling |

Every doubling of the horizon costs four times the radius and two levels of
depth.

#### Growing the planet on its own makes it worse

| World | Horizon | Relief in it | Worst | Gradient |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R 1,700 m, D11, heightScale 120 | 74 m | 17.7 m | 46.5 m | 0.239 |
| R 6,800 m, D13, heightScale 120 | 147 m | **10.8 m** | 33.2 m | **0.073** |
| R 6,800 m, D13, heightScale 480 | 147 m | 41.5 m | 116.7 m | 0.281 |
| R 6,800 m, D13, heightScale 720 | 147 m | 62.3 m | 172.4 m | 0.422 |

Noise is sampled from unit direction vectors, so a frequency is features per
sphere rather than per metre. Quadrupling the radius makes every landform four
times longer in metres while `heightScale` still says 120 m, and the horizon
only doubles, because it goes as the square root of the radius. The result is
**half the slope in view, on a planet that takes four times as long to cross**.

So the amplitude has to rise with the radius, and how far is the question step 2
answers.

#### Candidates

**A — heightScale 480.** Restores today's steepness at twice the viewing
distance. Relief in view 41.5 m, worst 116.7 m, gradient 0.281.

**B — heightScale 720.** Visibly mountainous: relief in view 62.3 m, worst
172.4 m, gradient 0.422 — a slope of 23 degrees on average across the horizon.

**Rejected, and recorded so nobody tries it again:** the radius alone at
heightScale 120. It halves the visible slope.

#### What depth 13 drags along

- **Address 33 bits**, and 55 of 64 with the planet and layer fields. Comfortable.
- **The crust cap flips.** The taper would allow 1,740 layers at D13, but the
  10-bit layer field allows 1,024, so the addressing binds where the taper bound
  at D11. 1,024 m of crust is ample for terrain reaching a few hundred metres.
- **Chunk level moves 6 to 8.** At D13 with chunk level 6 a chunk is `m = 128`,
  8,385 slots and 128 m across — fifteen times today. At chunk level 8 it is
  back to `m = 32`, 561 slots and 32 m, which is what every number Project 16
  measured assumes.
- **F-010 gets worse.** The level-of-detail chain bottoms out at the twenty
  icosahedron faces however far away the camera is. On a planet four times
  larger the floor is four times coarser in angle and the wasted generation
  grows with it. Pulled in to be decided alongside the radius rather than after.

#### Trial

Not started. A demo standing on the ground at both amplitudes, and `bench` run
at the new radius and depth to check the chunk numbers still hold.

### I-4 — A planet is not described in one place

**From:** step 1 of I-1, on the observation that the next world may be Mars-like
**Step:** 1 — proposed, not accepted

#### The problem

There is no single object that says what a world is. The numbers are spread
across a client file, a shader, and three engine modules: `RADIUS`, `DEPTH`,
`MAX_ELEVATION`, `COARSE_LEVEL`, `CLOUD_HEIGHT`, `CLOUD_LEVEL`, `SKIRT_CELLS`,
`DETAIL`, the terrain options, and seven atmosphere constants inside WGSL.

Every other item in this release changes one of those numbers, and each of them
lives somewhere different.

#### Why it is here

Not a fix on its own. It is what makes the other items land somewhere rather
than adding more constants to more files, and it is what a second world needs
before it can exist at all.

#### Candidates

Not named. The item is offered rather than accepted, and naming candidates for
work that may not happen wastes the naming.

### I-5 — The coarse map's resolution is tied to the planet's size

**From:** step 1 of I-3, working out what depth 13 costs
**Step:** 1 — proposed, not accepted

#### The problem

The coarse map is built at a subdivision level, and a level means a different
spacing on a different planet.

| World | Level | Cells | Spacing | One field |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R 1,700 m | 7 | 163,842 | 16 m | 0.6 MB |
| R 6,800 m | 7 | 163,842 | **64 m** | 0.6 MB |
| R 6,800 m | 9 | 2,621,442 | 16 m | 10.0 MB |

I-3 makes the planet four times larger, so the map that carries continents,
rivers and lakes drops to a quarter of its resolution against the ground.
Holding 16 m spacing costs level 9, ten megabytes a field, and world creation
rising from about one second to roughly sixteen — which is a loading screen, and
[doc 29](../docs/29-what-runs-where.md) has the client regenerating this map
rather than downloading it.

Whether rivers need 16 m spacing or read perfectly well at 64 m is unmeasured.

#### Why it is here

It is a decision with its own trial — `rivers.js` already measures river length
and how many reach the sea — and folding it into I-3 would smuggle a sixteen
second load into an item about landforms.

#### Candidates

Not named. Offered rather than accepted.
