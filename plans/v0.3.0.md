# v0.3.0

Minor: the height map. One map, and the world is it.

Taken to step 3 directly at the owner's instruction — the shape was settled in
the request rather than by a trial, so the discussion and the trials
[`HOW-TO-WRITE-PLANS.md`](../HOW-TO-WRITE-PLANS.md) asks for were skipped and
the measurements below were taken while building.

**Every existing seed produces a different world.** Nothing is saved yet, so
nothing is lost.

---

## What was wrong

Twelve knobs decided the ground and three of them were invisible.

`heightScale` multiplied the map into metres, `detailAmplitude` laid a second
noise field over it, and `detailFeature` set that field's size. None of the
three moved the picture the editor drew, so setting one meant walking the world
to find out what happened — and each one moved the crust budget, so raising one
meant lowering another to keep the sea floor from falling out of the bottom of
the world. On top of that the map itself was described by six numbers with the
same problem between them: a continent frequency and octave count, a relief
frequency and octave count and amplitude, and a land fraction, whose *ratios*
decided the coast.

---

## What it is now

**One field of octave noise, stated the way the reference implementation states
it.** Seed, noise scale, octaves, persistence, lacunarity, offset X and Y. Then
sea level at the percentile that leaves the asked-for land above it, the ground
scaled so its tallest point stands `relief` metres up, and water run over it.

**The map is stored in metres above sea level**, so sea level is zero by
construction and there is nothing to subtract. `TerrainGenerator.columnAt` reads
a height off the map and adds nothing — no noise, no multiplier, no second tier.

**Rivers and lakes are not generated.** The flow field, the water field, the pit
filling and the flow routing are all gone, along with the stream-power erosion
that needed them. Water is wherever the map is under zero.

## Items

### I-1 — Twelve knobs decide the ground and three are invisible

#### What was built

`octaveNoise` takes seed, frequency, octaves, persistence, lacunarity and a two
component offset, and gives `[-1, 1]`. Every octave gets its own offset hashed
from the seed: without one, two octaves of the same seeded lattice share their
zero crossings wherever their frequencies land near a whole multiple of each
other, and the ground grows a faint repeating grain.

`noiseHeight` runs it over the grid. `metreHeight` subtracts sea level and
scales so the tallest point is exactly `relief` metres — which makes that knob
*"how tall is the highest mountain"* rather than a multiplier on however high
this seed's noise happened to reach, so two seeds at one setting give two worlds
of the same stature.

The three landforms that are not plain noise — warped, grown, plates — take the
same seven numbers and lay them on their own structure, so the panel has one set
of noise knobs whichever is chosen.

#### The measurement that removed the detail term

The detail tier existed because a map cell is coarser than a block, so between
two samples the ground is one straight ramp. On the shipped map that ramp is 32
blocks long, and over every land cell:

| Land slope | Metres of rise across one 32 m map cell |
|---|---|
| median | 4.0 |
| 90th percentile | 9.1 |
| 99th percentile | 27.1 |
| steepest | 78.5 |

**A 32-block run rising 4 m is one block of climb every eight**, which reads as
a hillside rather than as a facet. The place a ramp shows is the steep 1%, and
the answer there is Map cell — a knob that moves the picture — not a term that
does not.

#### What refuses instead

The narrowest octave is `scale / lacunarity^(octaves−1)`, and the panel refuses
a map too coarse to carry it. **That one refusal replaces three knobs balanced
against each other**: ground the map cannot draw is ground the world does not
have, so it is refused rather than built invisibly.

The crust refusal was rewritten for the same reason. The sea floor's depth
depends on Land, because sea level is a percentile: measured on two landforms at
level 6, the whole ground span came to `1.69` times Relief at a land fraction of
`0.8`, `2.18` at `0.5`, `2.69` at `0.3` and `4.71` at `0.1`. The guess is a
curve through those with room over each.

### I-2 — Erosion needs the whole drainage network before it can cut

#### What was built

`erodeDroplets`. A droplet starts on a hashed cell and walks downhill cell to
cell, cutting where it is moving fast and depositing where it slows. No routing,
no pit filling, no stored flow — it looks only at the cell it stands on and the
six around it.

#### Two constants, both found by measuring the wrong answer

**Capacity is a gradient, never a fall in metres times a cell width.** With the
second form a droplet crossing flat ground on a 100 m map wanted to carry `15 m`
of material and cut it out, so erosion moved **15 m per cell** before doing
anything useful.

**A droplet may take a tenth of one step's fall and no more.** Uncapped it takes
a whole step at once and leaves a pit for the next droplet to fall into:
uncapped erosion **multiplied the median slope by four and the 90th percentile
by seven**, which is the opposite of what water does to a hillside.

> Level 7, 100 m cells, 300 m of relief, slope in metres of fall per metre.
>
> | Erosion | median | 90th | 99th | steepest | ground moved |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | 0 | 0.077 | 0.144 | 0.209 | 0.30 | — |
> | 0.1 | 0.078 | 0.149 | 0.223 | 0.46 | 1.07 m |
> | 0.3 | 0.078 | 0.160 | 0.298 | 0.92 | 2.89 m |
> | 0.6 | 0.080 | 0.178 | 0.452 | 1.08 | 5.27 m |
> | 1 | 0.083 | 0.208 | 0.577 | 1.24 | 8.04 m |

The median barely moves and the tail grows, which is a channel network being cut
into ground that is otherwise left alone.

**It ships off.** `erosion` defaults to `0` and its row is off the panel, because
what the droplets cut is a lattice pattern rather than valleys — **F-039**, found
by hillshading an eroded map. The pass returns on its first line at zero, so the
worlds this release ships are the noise and nothing else. `?erosion=0.5` still
reaches it.

**This closes F-017**, whose complaint was that `erosionRate` meant a different
amount of cutting on every planet size and coarse spacing. Heights are metres
and the grid is metres, so every constant means something on the ground.

**And it closes F-030 for now** — *nobody has decided whether this game has
rivers* — by deciding that this release does not have them. The design stays in
doc 21 for whoever revisits it.

### I-3 — The map carries four fields and three of them draw nothing

`water` held sea level over the ocean and a lake surface over a flooded basin;
`flow` counted cells draining through each one; `slope` was the largest drop to a
neighbour. All three are gone, and with them `fillPits`, `routeFlow`,
`accumulateFlow`, `downhillOrder`, `MinHeap`, `coarseSlope` and the old `erode`.

**Slope was the last out and the clearest case.** It had exactly one reader in
the whole engine — a material rule turning ground past `0.38` of gradient into
bare stone — and that reader cost a `2.5 MB` field for one boolean test. Worse,
`slopeAt` interpolates the *map's* gradient, so a block was told how steep its
32 m map cell is rather than the ground under its feet, and the rock came out in
patches the size of map cells instead of as a cliff face. The rule went with the
field.

**One field, two pictures.** `CoarseField` gains an `id` and a `stage`, so two
entries may read one array and stop at different points in the build:

| Pane | Stops at | Shows |
|---|---|---|
| Height | the metre scale | the ground the noise makes, before water |
| Ground | erosion | the finished surface, in the world's own colours |

**The build is three steps, not five**: the noise, the metre scale, erosion.

### I-6 — A slider can be dragged into a refusal

**From:** the owner, 2026-08-18 — *"I get this very often and I spend a lot of
time to just tweak the sliders"*
**Step:** 3, taken directly

Several knobs bound each other, and every pair was found by hitting it. The
crust has to reach past the ground, a map cell has to be coarser than a block,
the narrowest octave has to be wider than two map cells, a chunk has to be
smaller than a face, and the high cloud deck has to be over the low one.

`PlanetSettings.rangeFor` returns a knob's range narrowed by the rest of the
draft, and `settle` pulls every value inside one, in dependency order. The
slider's own end moves, the row says whose end it is, and the track turns amber
so a narrowed knob is visible before it is dragged. **The narrowing only moves an
end inward and `settle` only pushes a value into range**, so lowering Relief
widens what the crust may be and leaves the crust where it was — nothing a person
set is undone by a knob they went on to turn back.

A test sweeps every knob to both ends of its own live range, settles, and asserts
`problems()` is empty. The refusals stay, reachable only from a hand-edited query
string.

**One of them was also describing physics that does not exist.** *"The sea floor
would fall through the bottom of the world"* — nothing falls. `blockAt` returns
air below the last layer, so a column whose ground sits under the crust is water
all the way down with no seabed at all. The message says that now.

### I-9 — A world can open at a pole

**From:** the owner, 2026-08-18
**Step:** 3, taken directly

The opening view was the highest ground the chunk atlas found anywhere, which
put the shipped seed down at `47.6°N`. Two reasons a pole is the wrong place to
start. An equirectangular map smears a polar row across its whole width, so a
player standing there cannot find themselves on the picture beside them; and
[doc 20](../docs/20-player-coordinates.md) puts an icosahedron vertex at each
pole, which is a pentagon and the one cell shape nothing else on the planet has.

The search now skips anything outside **30 degrees** of the equator. That band
holds ten of the twelve pentagons and neither pole, and it has land on every
world: sampled across eight noise seeds it runs from `19.5%` land to `37.7%`.
The shipped seed opens at `29.97°N`.

### I-11 — The map only redraws once the knob is let go

**From:** the owner, 2026-08-18
**Step:** 3, taken directly

The pane waited `250 ms` for a slider to stop before building anything, which
meant the picture appeared after the hand let go rather than under it. Acting on
every step instead is worse the other way: a build is a second or more and a
dragged slider fires dozens of times, so they queue faster than any of them
finishes.

**One build in flight, and the newest values always next.** A change during a
build is remembered rather than queued, and the build that lands starts it. What
is drawn keeps up with the hand to within one build, and no work is done for a
value already replaced. Measured by watching the canvas through a four-second
drag of Noise scale: **12 distinct maps while the knob was still moving**, and
the last one is the value it landed on. Software-adapter timings; a real machine
draws more.

A level change still waits, because it is a new grid rather than a rebuild —
tens of megabytes, and dropping one to build another is what ran a worker out of
memory the first time a slider was dragged.

### I-12 — The warp has two constants and neither reaches the panel

**From:** the owner, 2026-08-18
**Step:** 3, taken directly

The `warped` landform displaces its sample point by a second noise field before
reading the first, and how far it pushes and how wide that field is were engine
constants nobody could turn. The trial that priced the four landforms said so at
the time — *"close to a no-op at the one setting tried"* — and then nobody swept
it.

Both are sliders now, greyed out on the other three landforms because they reach
nothing there. Warp scale is stated in metres like every other feature size, so
`1.6` features per sphere became `4,250 m` on the shipped radius.

**Swept, it has range the default does not use.** Land fraction is a percentile,
so it is identical at every setting and what moves is how long a coast is for the
same amount of land. At level 7 on one seed:

| Warp | coast edges | separate land bodies | land |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 6,989 | 9 | 30.0% |
| 0.35, the old constant | 8,203 | 14 | 30.0% |
| **0.8**, the default | 13,246 | 40 | 30.0% |
| 1.5 | 19,182 | 82 | 30.0% |

`0.35` added **17%** of coast, which is the "nobody would see it" the trial
measured, so the default is **`0.8`** — `90%` more coast and forty landmasses
against nine. The top of the slider adds `175%`.

### I-13 — A teleport drops the player, and the globe needs a mouse

**From:** the owner, 2026-08-18
**Step:** 3, taken directly

Three changes to the globe and what pointing at it does.

**The default landform is `warped`.** With Warp at `0.8` it is the only one of
the four that gives a ragged coast without giving up the land fraction as a
number that can be asked for, which is what `grown` charges for it.

**A teleport keeps the height above the ground, not the radius.** Somebody
looking at the planet from 2,000 m up wants to arrive still looking at it; a
radius kept across a teleport instead would bury them inside the first mountain
taller than the one they left. Measured: flying to `524 m` and then teleporting
three times landed at `439 m` every time — the same clearance, differing from
`524` only by the `85 m` of ground under the place they left. A standing player
keeps standing, because their clearance is already the `1.2 m` floor.

**A held finger picks, where a right-click does.** A finger has no second
button. A press held still for `450 ms` without wandering more than `10 px`
picks the place under it; any movement past that cancels, which is what stops a
slow drag from teleporting somebody. Long press is offered on touch and pen
only — a mouse keeps right-click, because a mouse press held still is how a slow
drag begins. The browser's own context menu is swallowed either way, so a long
press does not raise a menu over the ball.

### I-14 — The world is hills, and Relief is capped by the crust

**From:** the owner, 2026-08-18, with two photographs — a green dome and an
alpine range — and *"Relief slider is bound to crust, I don't feel that's the
right approach"*
**Step:** 3, taken directly

Two problems, and only one of them was the one that looked obvious.

**Steepness was never it.** Measured on the shipped map before anything changed:
median land gradient `11.1°`, 90th `25.2°`, 99th `38.1°`, steepest `56.0°`. That
is steep ground, and it still read as hills — because a sum of smooth functions
is smooth, so every summit was a dome. A mountain ridge is a **crease**, and the
only place a crease can come from is an absolute value. `ridge` folds each octave
at its own zero crossing with `1 - |n|`, squares the fold to sharpen it, and
weights each ridged octave by the one above so the flats stay flat. At `0.6` the
median goes to `24.3°` and the 99th to `63.7°`. **At `0` the field is
bit-for-bit what it was**, so nothing is taken away.

**And the owner was right about the cap.** Relief was bound to the crust because
the crust had to span the whole field, and the field's sea floor ran `1.92x`
deeper than its peaks were tall — 300 m of relief put the floor at `-575 m` and
spent `942 m` of a 1,024-layer budget. The ocean was taking twice the mountains'
share for ground [doc 25](../docs/25-water.md) never draws. Land and sea are
scaled apart now, each to its own knob, so the span is `relief + seaDepth` and
Relief's ceiling goes from **320 m to 900 m** at a 1 m block.

Two new rows in the map pane: **Ridges** at `0.6` and **Sea depth** at `120 m`.

### I-15 — One noise function, and two landforms nobody wants

**From:** the owner, 2026-08-19 — *"I want you to make it possible to select
which type of noise function to use"*, naming Perlin, OpenSimplex2, psrdnoise
and cellular, with *"if a knob is irrelevant for the noise function do not
render that knob"*, and *"we can remove plate and grown heights, I don't find
them very useful"*
**Step:** 3, taken directly

**The octave stack now takes a basis.** One octave was value noise and nothing
else; it is now one of five, chosen by a **Noise** dropdown at the top of the
map pane. The other four are ports: Perlin with cube-edge gradients, OpenSimplex2
on its body-centred cubic lattice, psrdnoise with gradients that turn, and
cellular distance to scattered feature points. Every one is a function of a
point and a seed returning `[-1, 1]`, so frequency, octaves, persistence,
lacunarity, offset and ridge mean the same thing under all five and the sea-level
percentile downstream never learns which was used.

**The same frequency did not mean the same feature size, and that was a bug in
the slider rather than a fact to write down.** Measured as zero crossings along
an 8,000-unit walk at frequency 1, one feature runs `1.99` units in value noise
and `0.78` in psrd — so a **Noise scale** of 4,500 m drew continents 4,500 m
across in one basis and `1,800 m` across in another. Each basis's frequency is
multiplied by its own width over value noise's, which brings all five within
`0.9%` of each other. It matters past the label: the panel refuses a map too
coarse to carry the narrowest octave and works that width out from Noise scale
and lacunarity alone, so uncorrected the refusal was right for one basis and
wrong for four.

**Three knobs are new and none of them is shown where it means nothing.** Rows
now come off the panel rather than greying out: **Spin** appears only under psrd,
**Cells** and **Jitter** only under cellular, and **Warp scale** only once Warp
is above zero. Under value noise the pane holds exactly the rows it held before.

**Landform stopped being a dropdown.** `noise` and `warped` differed by one
number — the warp amplitude — and `grown` and `plates` are gone, so the setting
is gone with them and `warpAmplitude: 0` is the plain field. That trades one
dropdown for another rather than adding one, and `surfaceHeight` replaces four
files with one. **The shipped default world does not move**: value noise has a
frequency multiplier of exactly 1 and the warp still defaults to `0.8`.

**What it cost is one determinism guarantee, for one basis.** psrdnoise builds
each gradient by rotating a hashed direction and a rotation is a sine, which is
a library routine rather than an IEEE operation. There are only `289` hashed
indices, so the whole trigonometric part is four tables of 289 entries built
once and nothing on the sampling path computes a sine — but two runtimes may
still fill those tables a bit apart. **F-041** records it; the other four bases
are unaffected.

### I-16 — There is nothing between grass and snow

**From:** the owner, 2026-08-19 — *"Add a rock material between grass and snow"*,
after a frame of green ground running to the horizon
**Step:** 3, taken directly

**A rock line, and it is an elevation.** Under it the soil is grass over dirt;
over it the soil is gone and the stone the ground is made of shows, through the
whole soil band rather than only its top layer, so a hillside that high is rock
where it is cut into as well as where it is walked on. Over the snow line one
layer of snow lies on that same rock. `BlockType.STONE` was already there with a
grey already defined; nothing new was added to the palette.

**Where the line goes came from how much land it leaves each band.** Land
elevation over relief on the shipped world runs median `0.21`, 90th `0.51`, 99th
`0.75`. The snow line at `0.72` leaves **1.5%** of land above it, which is a
speck and was the only thing standing between a green swell and white — hence
the complaint. A rock line at **`0.45`** puts 14.9% above it, so rock takes
**13.4%** of the land. On a world with more land they grow together: at a land
fraction of `0.65` the same two lines give 23.3% rock and 4.4% snow. Both are
fractions of relief, so the shares hold whatever height a world is stated at.

**The rule this replaces was a slope and it is not coming back.** Ground past a
cliff gradient came out as bare stone, which carried a `2.5 MB` slope field for
one boolean test and read the **map cell's** gradient rather than the block's,
so its rock arrived in patches the size of map cells. An elevation needs no
field: the column already knows how high it stands.

**What it exposed:** the Ground map paints in absolute metres with grey rock at
`300 m`, and the world picks materials as fractions of relief. The two agree
only near a relief of `555 m`. **F-042**, not fixed here, because closing it
takes snow off the shipped world and that is a look to choose rather than a bug
to squash.
