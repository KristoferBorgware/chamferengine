# v0.4.1

Patch: the three findings v0.4.0 turned up, and a way to look at what the
selection is deciding.

Every item here comes from something observed while building the edit path.
Nothing adds a capability to the world; the one new control draws what the
engine is already doing so a judgement about it can be made by looking.

## I-1 — The selection reads the ground the seed made

**From:** [F-067](../FINDINGS.md)
**Step:** 1

### The problem

`ChunkPeaks` holds the tallest and the lowest ground under every triangle of
the chunk hierarchy, built once from the coarse map. The map is a picture of the
**generated** world: no placed block is in it and none can be.

**The finding understated what that costs.** It is not only a level-of-detail
question. `selectChunks` reads the peak three times, and two of them decide
whether a chunk is drawn at all:

- **Over the horizon.** A chunk is dropped when its own ground cannot poke back
  round the curve. A tower's chunk is judged by the hillside under it.
- **Out of view.** The chunk gets a bounding ball stretched from its own lowest
  ground to its own highest, and a ball that misses the frustum prunes the
  triangle **and all four children under it** in one test. A build standing out
  of the top of that ball is outside the volume that decides its fate.
- **How far away.** The distance is measured to the chunk's ground, which then
  picks the level of detail.

So a tall build can be **culled outright**, not merely coarsened: the ball is
sized to the seed's hillside, the tower stands out of the top of it, and when
the hillside leaves the screen the tower goes with it.

### Candidates

**A — The store raises the pyramid.** A chunk holding changes reports its
highest placed block and its lowest, and `ChunkPeaks` takes the larger of that
and the map's figure, up through every ancestor. The store already knows which
chunks those are; a layer is a radius, so the metres are arithmetic. Costs one
number per changed chunk, maintained on write, and a pass over the store when a
world is opened.

**B — A chunk holding changes is never culled.** Keep the pyramid as it is and
exempt edited chunks from the horizon and frustum tests. Simpler, and it gives
up the pruning that makes the walk cheap: a triangle that cannot be refused
takes its four children with it, so a scattering of edits across a planet
un-prunes a large part of the hierarchy.

## I-2 — Every click rewrites the whole world's record

**From:** [F-068](../FINDINGS.md)
**Step:** 1

### The problem

The browser's database holds one record per world, carrying every chunk's
changes together, so writing one block packs and stores all of them. It grows
with the size of the world rather than with the size of the change.

### Candidates

**A — One record per chunk.** Keyed by the world's name and the chunk's key
together, with the header in a record of its own. A click writes one chunk;
opening a world is a range query over its name. This is the shape
[`ARCHITECTURE.md`](../ARCHITECTURE.md) already commits to for the hosted store
— chunk ID to a blob, one `get` and one `put` — so it is that migration arriving
early rather than a new idea.

**B — Write on a timer rather than on every click.** Keep one record per world
and save at most a few times a minute, plus once when the page is hidden. Fewer
writes for the same shape, and it trades a durable click for a cheaper one: a
browser closed at the wrong moment loses the last few seconds of building.

## I-3 — A change is written into two or three chunks and nothing says so

**From:** [F-069](../FINDINGS.md)
**Step:** 1

### The problem

A chunk draws only the cells it owns — the border rule settles that, and the
mesher enforces it. But to decide whether to put a face on the edge of a cell it
does own, a chunk has to know whether the neighbour is solid, and that neighbour
may belong to the chunk next door.

**For generated terrain this is not a problem, and the reason is exact.** The sampler does not fetch the neighbour's data. It re-evaluates the
column from the seed, and gets the same blocks the neighbour's own array holds,
because terrain is a pure function of the address. Every chunk computes the same
answer independently, so they cannot disagree and nothing has to be shared.

**An edit is the first thing in the world that is not a function of the
address.** Re-evaluating gives the ground the seed made, not the wall a player
built, so the assumption underneath the border rule stops holding. A change is
therefore written into every chunk whose triangle contains the cell, and 17% of
a chunk's slots sit on a border.

What is left is that the store is a few per cent larger than the number of cells
anybody changed, and that `count` reports records rather than cells — neither
wrong, both easy to read as a bug.

### Candidates

**A — Report both.** A `cells` beside `count`, and the duplication stated where
it is written. Keeps the read path as it is: a chunk's records arrive with the
job and need no gathering.

**B — Store canonically and gather at the job.** One record per cell, in the
chunk that owns it, and a job carries its chunk's rows plus those of the chunks
sharing its rim. Smaller store, one place to write, and it moves work to the
read — every job now assembles three rows instead of one, and each record has to
carry which chunk it was written under.

## I-4 — Nothing draws the volumes the culling tests against

**From:** the owner, judging the selection by eye
**Step:** 1

### The problem

Two different balls decide whether a chunk reaches the screen, and neither is
visible from the camera that decides them.

The **selection's** ball is built in `selectChunks` from the triangle's own
tallest and lowest ground, widened by a margin that grows with distance. It
decides whether a chunk is asked for at all.

The **patch's** ball comes back with the mesh — the ball the built geometry
actually fits inside — and decides whether a resident chunk is drawn this frame.

The selection reads pessimistic and there is no way to see where the slack is:
in the margin, in the peak the triangle is credited with, or in the ball's own
shape. A frame of the balls answers that directly, the same way the frozen view
marker answers what the selection could see.

### Candidates

**A — Two switches, one per volume.** Each draws its balls as three rings on the
axes, at the chunk's own level, over the world and inside its depth. Two rather
than one because the question is usually which of the two is refusing a chunk,
and a picture with both in it does not answer that.

**B — One switch and a colour per volume.** Fewer rows on the panel and both
volumes always drawn together. Reads as clutter at any distance, since a
selection is hundreds of chunks and each ball is a metre of ring against a
kilometre of ground.

## I-5 — The margin beyond the view is metres on every chunk

**From:** I-4's picture, and the owner reading it
**Step:** 3 — **built**

### The problem

The culling keeps a margin of ground beyond the edge of the screen, so turning
finds it already built. **That margin was never applied to the view.** The cull
tested the camera's own 65 degrees, and `selectChunks` added the margin's
tangent times the distance to **each chunk's own ball**.

So "keep 25 degrees beyond the screen" was spelled as "make every chunk's test
volume much larger than the chunk". Drawing the volumes is what showed it: the
rings were so large they read as straight lines across the whole view.

> **[measured]** `tools/trial-bounds.ts`, on the shipped world from where a
> player stands. The ball each chunk was culled by ran **8.2×** the half-width
> of the chunk inside it, at every level of detail, and **76.4%** of the average
> radius was the margin — 190 m of it, against 30 m for the triangle's own width
> and 29 m for its floor-to-peak span.

Three things follow, and only the first is obvious. A ball is a sphere, so 8.2×
the radius is **512× the volume**, and every cubic metre of it votes to be kept.
The margin grows with distance, so a chunk a kilometre out wears a 466 m coat.
And it is added in **every** direction, including at the near plane, the far
plane and behind the camera, where no margin was ever wanted.

### What it is now

The cull frustum is built from a **widened projection** and the ball is the cap
of ground the triangle holds and nothing else.

The vertical angle is widened enough that the horizontal one grows by the margin
too. The aspect ratio turns a vertical widening into a smaller horizontal one,
and the sides are where turning brings ground on, so taking the vertical figure
alone would keep less to the sides than the knob asks for.

> **[measured]** Same script and same viewpoint. The ball against its own
> chunk's half-width falls from **8.2×** to **1.13×** at lod 3 and about 1.2×
> typically, and the selection from **946** chunks to **876**. The margin still
> does its job: **631** chunks are on screen and it keeps **245** more.

What is left of the radius is the ground: **50.9%** the triangle's own width and
**49.1%** half its floor-to-peak span.

**The finest chunks are still credited with more span than they have**, because
the pyramid holding those figures stops two levels above the map. That is
[F-070](../FINDINGS.md), left deliberately: the default world is cut one level
below the cap so it barely bites, and closing it costs 7 MB against the 437 KB
the table takes now.

## I-6 — A chunk is a wedge, and a ball cannot hold one

**From:** the owner, reading I-4's picture and asking what happens when a
player digs
**Step:** 3 — **built**

### The problem

Everything a chunk is tested against was a **ball**: the volume `selectChunks`
tests before asking for a chunk, the volume `ChunkRenderer` tests before drawing
a resident one, and the volume `CascadeShadow` tests before drawing one into a
shadow map. A ball is a fine fit for the ground, which is a thin cap a few tens
of metres across and a few tens of metres tall.

**It is the wrong shape for the world the chunk actually holds.** A chunk is a
small triangle on the surface extruded straight down through the crust, and how
far down depends on how deep anybody has dug. **A ball cannot be grown downward
alone.** Grow it to reach the bottom of a shaft and it reaches the same distance
sideways, upward and behind, and every cubic metre of that votes to be kept.

This is a voxel engine, so the shaft is not a corner case. It is the second
thing a player does.

> **[measured]** `tools/trial-bounds.ts`, section 4, on the shipped world.
> A 76 m chunk over a 1,232 m crust. Holding just the ground it needs a box
> 44 m deep, and a ball round that is **3×** the volume. Dug a quarter of the
> way down it is **13×**. Dug to the bottom the ball is **640 m** in radius
> against a 76 m chunk — **148×** the volume of the box, all of it ground the
> cull can no longer refuse.

There was a second half to it, in the store rather than the geometry.
`DeltaStore` recorded how far a change reached so the selection could widen the
volume past what the coarse map knows, and it **left breaking out** on the
reasoning that taking ground away never puts geometry outside the volume the
ground already had. That is wrong: the walls of a hole are geometry standing
where the map says solid ground is. A player at the bottom of their own mine was
in a chunk whose test volume stopped at the surface far above them.

### What it is now

`Box` — a centre, three perpendicular unit axes and a half-width along each.
`Frustum.holdsBox` tests one against six planes the way `holds` tests a sphere,
with the box's reach along each plane's normal standing in for the radius.
`chunkWedge` builds the one a chunk wants: long axis along the chunk's own
direction from the planet's centre, from the inner surface's nearest corner out
to the outer surface, and both widths the radius of the disc the triangle sits
in.

Three volumes changed, not one.

- **The selection.** `ChunkCull` is a box test now, and `selectChunks` builds
  the wedge from the peak and trough the pyramid holds.
- **The renderer.** `ArrayMeshSink` measures itself along axes it is handed
  rather than the world's, so a `ChunkMesh` carries the box its own triangles
  fall inside — three dot products a vertex against three comparisons.
- **The shadow.** `CascadeShadow.boxOf` takes a box: how far it reaches along
  the light and how far it stands off the axis are each a sum over its three
  axes, a cosine for the first and a sine for the second. A shaft under a low
  sun barely widens the second.

And `DeltaStore` now stretches a chunk's reach for **every** change, breaking as
well as placing.

The diagnostic follows: `BoundsRenderer` draws each volume as its twelve edges
rather than three rings.

> **[measured]** Same script and viewpoint, undug ground. A ball round each
> box would be **3.8×** its volume at the finest level and 2.8× at the
> coarsest. That is what the shape is worth before anybody picks up a shovel.

### What it does not change

The margin is still an angle on the view (I-5), the peak pyramid still stops two
levels above the map ([F-070](../FINDINGS.md)), and the horizon test is still a
dot product against a direction. None of those is a shape question.
