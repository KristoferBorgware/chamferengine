Worked through in the three steps
[`HOW-TO-WRITE-PLANS.md`](../HOW-TO-WRITE-PLANS.md) sets out — discuss, try,
implement.

**Steps 1 and 2 were done in [`v0.3.0`](v0.3.0.md)** and are not repeated here.
The four candidates were named, built outside the engine and measured in
`verification/coastline.js`, and the measurements chose nothing: what separates
them is what a person wants a planet to look like. That is
[`HOW-TO-WRITE-PLANS.md`](../HOW-TO-WRITE-PLANS.md)'s case for putting every
side in behind a switch and looking at the real thing, and
[`v0.2.0`](v0.2.0.md)'s editor is what makes looking possible.

**This release is that switch.** The item leaves v0.3.0 and lands here, ahead of
the rest of the map work.

## I-1 — There is one way to decide where the land is

**From:** the owner, 2026-08-18 — the four were measured and none of them could
be looked at
**Step:** 3, and **judged in the engine**: the losing candidates come out of a
later release, once one has won.

### The four

| | raggedness | land fraction | largest landmass | longest river |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `noise` | 1.06–1.12 | exact | 27,305 | **172** |
| `warped` | 1.13–1.17 | exact | 26,913 | 153 |
| `grown` | **1.40–1.45** | near, not on | 14,910 | 83 |
| `plates` | 1.18–1.50 | exact | 25,072 | 94 |

Raggedness is the rate a coastline's length grows as the cells halve. A curve
carrying no detail below the map's own resolution doubles exactly, so `1.00` is
smooth and the excess is what ragged means as a number. Real coasts, quoted and
not measured here, run from about 1.05 for South Africa through 1.25 for Britain
to about 1.52 for Norway.

### Built

`landformHeight` is the one place the four are chosen between, so the two
builders cannot drift: everything downstream — sea level, erosion, routing, the
water surface — reads a height and never learns which of them made it.

**`plates` lays a plate out from hashed directions, never from angles.** A seat,
a rotation axis and a rate are each three hashed components divided by their own
length — a wrapping multiply and a square root, both pinned to the bit by IEEE
754. `sin` and `cos` would put a transcendental in a field two clients have to
agree on exactly. A draw landing near the middle is thrown out, because
normalising it would swing on its last bits.

**Its reach is a distance, so it doubles for every level finer than the one it
is stated at.** A fixed number of cells makes a range twice as wide on a map
drawn at half the resolution, and the same seed then grows different mountains
at different resolutions.

**`grown` needed two things the trial did not have.** A mask is not a height, so
the distance to the coast supplies one: ground rises inland and falls away
offshore, with relief over the top. **The shore has to be steep against that
relief** — at a gentle rise the relief decides where the water stops and the
grown mask is drowned by it, which showed up as a growth weight that changed
nothing across its whole range.

And the growth rule is a **majority** rather than a coin. Flipping a boundary
cell either way is symmetric: it adds as much noise as it removes, so the mask
never consolidates however hard the knob is turned. Measured at level 6, moving
to the majority takes the largest landmass from 1,805 cells to 4,458 and the
islands from 218 to 44 across the same range of that knob. The symmetric rule is
what the trial ran, and it is part of why the trial's `grown` came out as ragged
as it did.

**Its land fraction is still a request rather than an answer**, which is the one
thing this landform gives up. There is no percentile in the growth.

### Waiting on a look

Nothing here chooses. All four are in the panel under **Landform**, the map pane
redraws for it, and the release closes when one is picked and the other three
come out.
