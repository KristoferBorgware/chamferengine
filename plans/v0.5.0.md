## I-1 — A generated world has no caves

**From:** the owner, 2026-08-25
**Findings pulled in:** [F-078](../FINDINGS.md)
**Step:** 2

### The problem

`caveDensity` exists in `packages/engine/src/generation/terrain/`, and nothing
reaches it. `TerrainOptions.caves` defaults to `false` and no panel row sets it,
so **no shipped world holds a single cave**.

Behind that sit two separate rules under one name, which is
[F-078](../FINDINGS.md). Doc 08 and `verification/volume.js` argue a **signed
density** — solid where `(surfaceRadius − r) + noise × strength > 0` — whose
first term is a bias growing 1 per metre of depth, and that bias is where the
rule *an enclosed void needs the noise gradient to beat 1* comes from, along
with doc 14's `11×` face bill and `13–32%` multi-span columns. `caveDensity`
does neither: it is a **band around zero**, hollow where `|fbm| < threshold`,
with a hard refusal inside `caveCeiling` of the surface. So every cave number
the corpus holds describes a form the engine does not run, and the first attempt
to tune caves would be tuned against measurements taken of something else.

### Candidates

**A — a plan drawn in 2D, then extruded.** A passage is a band around zero of a
noise field over the **ground**, so the passages are that field's own contour
lines and they wind, branch and join. How far inside the band a column sits then
gives the roof height as well, so one lookup decides both the shape on the
ground and the shape across it. A floor depth per column puts the slab in the
crust. Several independently seeded fields may be laid over one another, because
one field's contours can never cross each other.

**B — the sheet, which is `caveDensity` as it stands.** A band around zero of a
field in **space**. The zero set of a field on a plane is a set of curves and
the band round one is a passage; the zero set of a field in space is a set of
**surfaces**, and the band round one is a slab. So what B carves is not a
network of corridors but one folded sheet running through the crust.

### Trial

[`demos/cave-lab.html`](../demos/cave-lab.html) carried both, switched by one
row, with the seed, the ground, the block size, the volume and every measurement
held still across the switch. One patch: **95 × 95 m** of ground, **28 m** of
crust, **1 m** blocks, **9,216** columns, seed `cave`.

| | A — plan | B — sheet |
|---|---|---|
| passage under | 25.0% of columns | **69.8%** |
| narrowest way through, median | 4 cells | **20 cells** |
| separate systems | 4 | **3** |
| largest system holds | 98.3% | **100%** |
| faces per column | **×2.71** | ×4.82 |
| noise lookups a column | **16.0** | 78.0 |

**Chosen: B.** The measurement that decides it is not in that table, because it
is a bound rather than a reading: **A gives each column exactly one cave slab,
so a passage can never cross over another passage.** No setting of A reaches a
second storey; the shape of the address forbids it. B has no such bound, and it
is what the engine already carries.

**What B costs is accepted rather than argued away.** It reads its field once a
**layer** where A reads once a column, which is the `78.0` against `16.0`; it
draws `×4.82` the faces of the same ground with no caves; and its passages are
caverns. **B has no setting that is both narrow and connected** — squeezed to a
3-cell median it shatters into **1,976** separate systems with the largest
holding **1.8%** of the void. If walkable corridors turn out to matter, B is not
the answer and this item reopens.

**What A cost, for whoever revisits it.** A's passages arrive speckled: an
octave whose own wobble is wider than the band drops isolated one-cell columns
along every passage, and each takes nearly a full roof, so a marginal cell
becomes a chimney several metres tall. Two corrections were built and both are
worse than the defect:

| A, at 3 octaves and a 0.05 band | passage under | one cell wide | systems | largest holds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| as it stood | 25.7% | 17.8% | 12 | 79% |
| roof read past a rarity of 0.4 | 16.3% | **44.7%** | **98** | **24%** |
| passages under 2 cells refused | 15.0% | 14.7% | **54** | **18%** |
| **one octave, 0.055 band** | **25.0%** | **11.4%** | **4** | **98.3%** |

Both corrections gate the cave on the field that **is** the cave, so they narrow
every passage, and a passage narrows first at its joins. Dropping the octaves
the band is too narrow to resolve fixes it instead, at the same cave volume and
20% less generation.

**The tool that judged them was an isosurface, and it is gone.** Marching
tetrahedra over the lattice's own triangular prisms — three columns and two
layer boundaries make a prism, a prism splits into three tetrahedra, and a
tetrahedron cut by a surface has either one corner on its own side or two, so
there is no ambiguous case to guess at. It is what showed A's chimneys. It draws
no blocks, and a world of hexagons has no use for it past that.

### What still blocks shipping caves

[F-025](../FINDINGS.md) stays open and is the reason this item is not the whole
job. A cave mouth crossing a level-of-detail join is a horizontal hole in a wall
below anything a curtain hangs from: the apron closes all **76** surface slits
and **none** of the **1,074** cave mouths, **99%** of which sit deeper than any
curtain reaches. Nobody sees it while caves are off. The first release that
turns them on gets sky-through-the-planet back at exactly the joins v0.4.1
closed for the surface, so F-025 is either pulled into this release or caves
stay off in it.

### Implementation

Not started. Step 3 runs once for the whole release, and the scope is not cut.

The work this item names: `caveDensity` keeps its band and gains the ceiling I-2
chooses; `verification/volume.js` gains a section measuring **the band** — face
cost, multi-span share, mouth count — because its section 3 measures the density
form; the gradient sentence comes off `caveDensity`'s own comment, since it
describes the bias term the function does not have; and the three knobs reach
the panel.

## I-2 — A cave has no way to the surface

**From:** I-1's trial, 2026-08-25
**Step:** 2

### The problem

`caveCeiling` is a yes or a no on one number. At its shipped **6 m** nothing
ever breaks the ground — **0** mouths over 9,216 columns. At **0 m** the sheet
is near the surface everywhere and opens it everywhere — **2,944** mouth
columns, 32% of the patch. **There is no setting between the two**, because the
gate has no way to say *here and not there*.

A cave nobody can walk into is a cave nobody meets.

### Candidates

**A — taper.** The band narrows toward the ground instead of stopping dead at
it, so only the part of the sheet nearest its own middle is still wide enough to
be a cave when it arrives. A mouth is then where the **cave** is strongest. One
knob, and no second field.

**B — a dipping ceiling.** The ceiling itself wanders over the ground and comes
down where a second field clears a rarity. A mouth is where the **ground**
allows one **and** the sheet happens to be there, which is two conditions rather
than one. One 2D lookup a column, and three knobs.

### Trial

The same patch and the same seed as I-1, on carve B.

| | constant ceiling | A — taper | B — dipping |
|---|---|---|---|
| mouths at the surface | 0 | 29 (0.31%) | **12 (0.13%)** |
| passage under | 67.8% of columns | 66.3% | 69.8% |
| separate systems | 4 | **124** | **3** |
| largest system holds | 73% | 73% | **100%** |
| faces per column | ×4.65 | ×5.19 | ×4.82 |
| extra lookups a column | — | **0** | 1 |

**Chosen: B.** The deciding number is the system count. A thins the sheet near
the surface everywhere, which shears the top off it: **124 separate systems
against 4**, with 27% of the void broken into pieces. B leaves the sheet exactly
as it was and puts holes in its roof, so a mouth is a way into the cave that was
already there rather than a new pocket. A's one advantage — no second lookup —
buys a worse cave and 7% more faces.

**Two limits, recorded because both are sharp.**

**Where the rarity starts is a knob and cannot be a constant.** Fixing it at
`0.5` on a two-standard-deviations argument opened half the ground: the `0.244`
figure belongs to a particular octave count, and over a patch this size the
field never sees its own full range — at a 60 m feature over 95 m of ground the
median reading is **0.461** and **46.5%** of the patch clears `0.5`.

**The dip is a step in miniature.** Ceiling dips of 5 m, 10 m and 15 m give
**0**, **12** and **346** mouths: once the dip is deep enough to reach the
surface anywhere, it reaches it across the whole dip region at once. 10 m is a
usable setting and either side of it is touchy.

### Implementation

Not started, with I-1.

The work: `caveDensity` takes the ceiling as a function of the column rather
than a constant, computed from one 2D field, one-sided so the ceiling only ever
comes down, and biting only above a rarity the panel sets. Setting the dip to
zero restores the constant ceiling exactly.
